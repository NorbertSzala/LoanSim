# Mathematical model: Phase 1 monthly convention

Source: `source-requirements.md` sections 8–10 and 23 and the owner's 2026-10-25 Phase 1 decisions recorded in `harness/decision-log.md`. Phase 1 is authorized. This document specifies the formulas and timing implemented by the core; observed checks are recorded separately in `harness/context/phase-01-financial-engine.md`.

## Approved model boundary

- Model twelve equal monthly periods per year. For an annual nominal rate `r`, the period rate is `r / 12`; calendar-day length and the displayed start date do not change Phase 1 interest. The entire initial principal is disbursed once before the first model period. An interest-accrual interface must permit a future, separately approved Actual/365 implementation.
- For opening principal balance `B` and the annual nominal rate applicable at that period's start, period interest is `ROUND_HALF_UP(B × r / 12, 0.01)`. Use `Decimal` with a fixed 50-significant-digit intermediate context before this single interest-rounding event. A zero-rate period has zero interest.
- For a fixed annual nominal rate, the simplified effective annual rate implied by monthly compounding is `(1 + r / 12)^12 - 1`. This is an informational rate conversion, not official RRSO and not a discounted-cash-flow measure. A variable path needs a separately approved reporting rule rather than one unexplained scalar.
- Apply a variable-rate change at the beginning of the specified model period. For an index-plus-margin path, preserve the index definition, supplied compounded-index value where applicable, margin, observation/publication and effective dates, and source or hypothetical-scenario label. Carrying forward the last known value for a future period is an expressly disclosed constant-rate scenario. Historical gaps cannot be filled as if observations existed.
- Distinguish mandatory installment, extra principal payment, and actual cash outflow. Allocate due additional charges, interest, scheduled principal, and then extra principal. Reconcile every payment row, initial principal, and final zero balance. A final payment is limited by the outstanding obligation; an installment or cap that does not cover interest must be detected.
- Separate principal, interest, mandatory financing charges, optional charges, and acquisition costs. A financed expense must be counted once. Distinguish interest, financing cost, lender repayments, and purchase-related outlay. Additional charges default to zero but have one-time/recurring and financed/own-funds metadata.
- If two modeled scenarios have identical actual cash flows, rates, period sequence, and fees, they must yield identical balances and interest costs. Interest savings from overpayments require an explicit comparable baseline.
- Fuller RRSO based on complete mandatory cash flows and dated discounting is deferred. Do not describe the simplified effective annual rate as RRSO, a lender offer, or a bank-exact result.

## Period, payment, and amount formulas

Represent an annual rate as a decimal fraction (for example, `0.12` for 12%). For each period `t`, use its opening cent balance `B[t-1]`, nominal annual rate `r[t]`, and `i[t] = r[t] / 12`. Set `I[t] = quantize_cent(B[t-1] × i[t], ROUND_HALF_UP)` exactly once. Do not use day counts. The full initial debt is available before period 1. Period 1 is displayed one calendar month after the start; each later date is generated from the original start and period number, with month-end anchoring.

The schedule calls an interest-accrual function with the opening balance, nominal rate, model-period start, and payment date. Its Phase 1 implementation ignores the date interval and applies the formula above. A later Actual/365 convention can supply another accrual function while preserving the schedule's allocation and balance reconciliation; its installment-recalculation method and date rules still require separate approval.

For `n` remaining periods and a constant current period rate `i`, calculate the unrounded annuity payment as `B × i / (1 - (1 + i)^(-n))`; for zero rate use `B / n`. Round that mandatory principal-and-interest payment to cents when it is established or recalculated. This is the ordinary-annuity present-value relationship also described by [OpenStax](https://openstax.org/books/principles-finance/pages/8-3-loan-amortization). An annual nominal rate change at a period boundary recalculates this mandatory payment over the remaining payoff target. When a term-shortening extra payment occurs, keep the current payment and set the target to the earliest period in which that payment would clear the new balance at the current rate. A later reset preserves that target and recalculates the payment. Under installment reduction, keep the original contractual target and recalculate the payment after the extra principal.

For declining payments, establish a rounded regular principal component from opening debt divided by term months. Interest varies with the opening balance and current rate. After a term-shortening extra payment, retain that regular principal component and bring the payoff date forward. Under installment reduction, recalculate the regular principal component from the remaining balance and months to the original contractual target. At a rate reset the interest component changes; the principal component remains as established. In both schedule types, the last mandatory payment is exactly the remaining balance plus due rounded interest if that is lower than the ordinary installment. No principal component or extra payment can reduce balance below zero.

In each period, determine due own-funds charges, rounded interest, mandatory principal, then extra principal. The actual scheduled payment includes the due charges and mandatory principal-and-interest. A combined-payment cap below the mandatory payment plus due charges is invalid; partial mandatory payments and negative amortization are unsupported. An extra payment plan may request a fixed regular amount, one-time amounts, or use the cap's remaining capacity as extra principal. If both a fixed request and a cap apply, use the smaller available extra amount. Record requested and applied extra amounts; cap the applied amount at the principal left after mandatory payment. Reconcile `opening balance - mandatory principal - applied extra = closing balance`, and reconcile each cash payment to its listed components.

## Rates, costs, and reported metrics

The rate plan supplies a nominal annual rate for every model period: fixed, periodically fixed with an explicit follow-on rule, or a monthly reference-index value plus margin. An observation for a historical model period must have been published no later than that period's displayed start and must identify its source. Missing observations inside a supplied historical/monthly path are errors. Beyond the last known observation, the last rate may continue only as a labeled constant-future-rate scenario. A supplied compounded POLSTR 1M index is used as the index value without re-compounding daily rates. Expose each period's nominal and simplified effective annual rate; a scalar summary is explicitly the initial-period rate.

One-time financed charges due at origination increase opening debt once at period 0. Own-funds one-time charges at period 0 are initial cash outflow; later charges are paid with the period's mandatory payment and before any extra principal. Monthly own-funds charges stop when the loan is discharged. A recurring charge marked as financed is rejected with a validation error; all-future-charge capitalization is excluded from Phase 1. Keep charge category (mandatory credit, optional credit, property acquisition), timing, funding, and payee distinct. Derive `financing cost = total nominal loan cost = total interest + mandatory credit charges + optional credit charges`; never add principal itself as a cost. `Total lender repayments` consists of repaid debt principal, interest, and charges actually paid to the lender outside financed principal. `Total cash outflow` includes modeled loan payments and own-funds charges, excluding property down payment; `lifetime buyer cash outflow` additionally includes the down payment. Each payment row's `actual_cash_outflow` includes due recurring charges and therefore represents the modeled monthly budget burden. `Total recurring charges` sums only incurred recurring charge events and is a breakdown already included in applicable cost and cash totals, not a further additive cost. Show property price plus acquisition charges and own-funds cash needed at acquisition separately so a financed charge is not added twice. Derive LTV from initial debt divided by property price when price is positive.

For each nominal annual rate `r[t]`, the simplified effective annual rate is `(1 + r[t] / 12)^12 - 1`. This conversion omits fees and dated discounting; never call it RRSO. Full RRSO is outside Phase 1. Overpayment interest savings compare against a separately calculated no-overpayment schedule with otherwise identical principal, rates, dates, and charges; do not attribute fee differences to interest savings.

Property and rate-component derivations preserve entered values and the provenance of each computed value. Money inputs must be exactly representable in cents; ratios are stored as `Decimal` fractions rather than rounded display percentages. Arithmetic is isolated from the caller's `Decimal` precision. A repeating ratio may leave a tiny residue when inverted to money; only a result within `(abs(rounded amount) + 1) × 1e-40` of an exact cent is accepted as that cent. Larger discrepancies and conflicting entered values still fail. An underdetermined set returns an explicit missing-value reason. A contradictory redundant set is rejected without overwriting any entered value. A fixed-rate annuity installment is derived from the specified initial debt, nominal rate, and term. A term is returned only if exactly one term through the explicit search bound (default 1,200 model months) produces the entered cent-rounded installment; a tied or nonmatching result is explained rather than guessed. Changing an input requires deriving again from immutable original inputs, preventing stale values and dependency cycles.

Every result carries calculation-convention identifier `monthly-nominal-12-half-up-v1` and an estimate notice that bank schedules may differ. Each payment row retains the applied rate object, including index, margin, source label, observation and publication dates where present. Each charge event retains category, payee, funding, period, and actual own-funds cash outflow. These fields allow later storage and reporting to reproduce the modeled result without reinterpreting an unlabeled rate or fee.

## Independent reference cases

Independent reference values must not call another LoanSim function as the oracle. For PLN 1,000.00, 12% nominal annual interest, and two annuity periods, the closed-form installment rounds to PLN 507.51; period 1 has PLN 10.00 interest and PLN 497.51 principal, leaving PLN 502.49; period 2 has PLN 5.02 interest and ends with a PLN 507.51 payment and zero balance. For two declining periods, principal portions are PLN 500.00 and PLN 500.00, interest is PLN 10.00 and PLN 5.00, and payments are PLN 510.00 and PLN 505.00. An independent published case in [OpenStax's amortization table](https://openstax.org/books/principles-finance-2e/pages/8-3-loan-amortization) uses a PLN-unit-independent principal of 32,000, a monthly rate of 0.005, 36 payments of 973.50, and a first-row interest/principal/balance of 160.00/813.50/31,186.50. These static references, zero-rate and short-term cases, rate resets, rounding boundaries, identical-cash-flow cases, and monetary invariants are covered by the Phase 1 tests. Calendar-day accrual and irregular periods remain outside Phase 1.
