# Financial assumptions register

Source: `source-requirements.md` §§8–10 and 23, followed by the owner's explicit Phase 1 modeling decisions and implementation authorization on 2026-10-25. These decisions specify an approximate, general mortgage simulator, not a bank-exact contract model. Phase 1 is authorized; Phases 2–7 remain locked.

## Owner-approved Phase 1 conventions

| Area | Approved convention |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Compare repeatable mortgage scenarios with disclosed assumptions. Results are estimates and may differ slightly from bank schedules. Do not claim to reproduce a named bank's contract. |
| Periods and interest | Model equal monthly periods and, by default, 12 installments per year. The period rate is annual nominal rate divided by 12. Loan-start date labels the schedule but does not alter interest. Disburse the entire initial loan amount once at the beginning. Do not use actual calendar-day accrual, holidays, or irregular periods in Phase 1. Keep the accrual boundary extensible for a future Actual/365 convention. |
| Money and rounding | Default currency PLN. Use `Decimal` for money, retain higher precision in intermediate work, and round settlement amounts to two decimal places with `ROUND_HALF_UP`. Round period interest once at the end of that period. Adjust the final payment to extinguish the actual remaining balance; document every rounding point and reconcile totals. |
| Rates | Variable annual nominal rate equals reference index plus bank margin. Accept a monthly rate path and apply a change at the beginning of its model period. Treat a supplied POLSTR 1M compounded-index value as an already calculated value; do not compound daily rates again. If a future path ends, carrying forward its last known value is an explicitly labeled constant-future-rate scenario, never a market forecast or historical observation. Historical inputs must not use information published after their effective date or fabricate missing observations. A periodically fixed model retains the protection length and explicit subsequent-rate rule; any unknown follow-on rate is a labeled hypothetical scenario. |
| Installments and overpayments | Support annuity and declining schedules; one-time and recurring extra principal payments; fixed extra amounts and a cap on combined monthly payment; and both term-shortening and mandatory-installment-reduction responses. Allocate due extra costs, then interest, scheduled principal, and finally extra principal. Apply extra principal after the mandatory installment. Default extra costs to zero. Omit early-repayment commissions and complex bank-specific settlement rules in Phase 1, while allowing later extension. Do not pay more than the outstanding obligation. Detect an installment or payment cap that fails to cover due interest. Identical actual dated cash flows, rates, and fees must yield identical balances and interest. |
| Cost categories | Keep loan principal, interest, mandatory credit charges, optional extra charges, and property-acquisition costs separate. Default additional charges to zero. Each charge identifies whether it is one-time or recurring and financed or paid from own funds. Report interest, financing cost, loan repayments, and property-purchase-related spending separately. Never count principal as financing cost or count a financed expense twice. |
| Rate metrics | Phase 1 reports annual nominal rate, a clearly labeled simplified effective annual rate under monthly compounding, nominal interest, other modeled financing charges, total repayments, and modeled repayment duration. Do not label the simplified rate as official RRSO. Defer fuller RRSO based on complete classified charges and dated discounted cash flows to a separately authorized later scope. |
| Verification | Document conventions and use unit, boundary, regression, and independent numerical-reference tests for material formulas. Do not advance to another phase without owner approval; no commit or push is authorized by these modeling decisions. |

## Owner-approved detailed Phase 1 choices

| Topic | Approved rule and comparison consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Recalculation | Term shortening preserves the current planned principal repayment pace after an extra payment. Installment reduction recalculates the mandatory payment over the remaining target term. A rate reset recalculates the mandatory installment using the remaining payoff target. This keeps mandatory obligations distinct from actual extra outflow. |
| Display dates | Show the first installment one calendar month after the start. Generate each later label from the original start date and period number, preserving a month-end anchor; never let a February clamp permanently move a 31st to the 28th. Display dates do not enter Phase 1 interest. |
| Further rounding | Settle mandatory installments, principal allocation, overpayments, charges, and the end-of-period balance to cents. Clear the remaining cent balance with the final payment. Retain extended precision only while computing an unrounded formula before a settlement event. |
| Arithmetic context | Use a fixed 50-significant-digit `Decimal` context for intermediate engine calculations so callers cannot change results by changing their own context; cent settlement uses `ROUND_HALF_UP`. |
| Rate paths | Require the first applicable rate. Reject missing observations inside a historical or supplied monthly path; do not backfill them. Carry the most recent known rate only into explicitly labeled future constant-rate scenario periods. Show the nominal and simplified effective rate per period; a summary value must be labeled as the initial rate. |
| Payment bounds | Reject a combined-payment limit or mandatory installment that cannot cover due charges and interest; do not model negative amortization. Cap an extra repayment to the amount needed to discharge the remaining debt and report requested and applied amounts separately. |
| Financed costs and totals | Finance only one-time charges due at origination. Add each such charge once to initial debt and LTV numerator; never add it again to borrower cash outflow at origination. Report acquisition-date outlay separately from lifetime buyer cash outflow. |
| Recurring charges | Model recurring charges only when paid from own funds. Show each due charge separately and include it in that period's actual cash outflow and any combined monthly-payment cap. Sum incurred recurring charges separately across the modeled payoff period; this breakdown is already included in applicable financing-charge and cash-outflow totals and must not be added to them again. If a recurring charge is marked as financed, reject the input with a clear validation error. Do not finance all future instances at origination. Financing a recurring charge when due is a possible later extension requiring a new decision. |
| Input breadth | Phase 1 supports only the approved one-time/regular fixed-amount and combined-payment-cap overpayments, PLN, and nonnegative total nominal rates. Defer percentage/custom-date/liquidity-limited schedules, promotional margin rules, other currencies, and negative-rate policy. |

## Owner decision on recurring-charge funding

On 2026-10-25 the owner selected variant A: Phase 1 finances only one-time origination charges. Recurring charges such as monthly insurance are own-funds expenses, included in the actual monthly budget burden without increasing loan principal. A recurring charge marked as financed is invalid rather than silently changed. The rejected alternative would have capitalized all future charges at origination and increased interest immediately. See the explicit approval in `harness/decision-log.md`.

The exact formulas, settlement timing, independent references, and refusal conditions are specified in `mathematical-model.md`. FIN-007 and FIN-008 acceptance cases are in `requirements-traceability.md`.
